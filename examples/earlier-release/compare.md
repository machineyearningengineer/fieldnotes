## Direct Comparative Evidence for Stationary Grid Storage

The provided literature does not report a direct empirical comparison between sodium-ion and lithium iron phosphate batteries specifically measuring cycle life and low-temperature performance in stationary grid storage systems\. Broader comparisons indicate that while sodium-ion batteries are considered potential candidates for stationary energy storage due to cost and safety advantages, they generally exhibit lower energy density and inferior cycle life compared to lithium-ion technologies, and they have not been established as competitive with high-energy systems like lithium iron phosphate cathodes in earlier evaluations\. [5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04963)

## Degradation Dynamics and Low-Temperature Limits in Lithium-Ion Systems

In generic lithium-ion configurations, operation under cold conditions such as 10 degrees Celsius exacerbates charge-transfer bottlenecks, accelerates lithium inventory loss, elevates overpotentials, and substantially reduces projected lifetime relative to moderate temperatures\. For stationary energy storage systems, optimal longevity relies on prioritizing calendar life and passive stability under mild C-rates of 0\.5C or below and ambient temperatures rather than aggressive cycling or extreme operating conditions\. [2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15440)

Experimental evaluations on large-format lithium iron phosphate pouch cells highlight that cycling degradation is strongly coupled to thermal gradients and cooling design, where 2C cycling over 103 cycles demonstrated that surface cooling reduces cell temperature rise and slows degradation compared to tab cooling configurations\. [4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10872)

## Cycle-Life Characteristics of Sodium-Ion Battery Chemistries

Sodium-ion battery technology faces cycle-life challenges tied to cathode degradation mechanisms, including large structural changes from volume expansion during sodium-ion \(de\)intercalation, Jahn-Teller distortions, and irreversible oxygen release accelerating electrolyte decomposition in layered transition metal oxides\. [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24683)

Phosphate-based polyanionic cathode materials offer robust structural frameworks that show extended cycle retention under laboratory testing, with specific compositions reaching thousands of cycles under varied rates, though practical stationary deployments depend heavily on addressing cell-level commercialization barriers\. [6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19299)
